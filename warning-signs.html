💬Need help now?

What to watch for, in yourself or a friend.

None of these signs mean something is definitely wrong. They're worth paying attention to, especially together.

Warning Signs What Helps

Possible signs

Feeling sad or hopeless

For a long stretch of time, not just one bad day.

Constant or excessive worry

Worry that doesn't ease up, even when things are okay.

Becoming withdrawn

Pulling away from friends, family, or things you used to do together.

Losing interest

Activities that used to feel good start feeling flat.

Trouble concentrating

Focus feels harder to hold onto than usual.

Changes in sleep

Sleeping a lot more, or a lot less, than normal.

Difficulties at school

Grades or attendance slipping in a way that's new.

Feeling lonely

Even when surrounded by people.

Major mood changes

Bigger, faster swings than what feels typical for you.

Everyday things feel harder

Getting up, getting dressed, replying to texts, all feel heavier.

One sign isn't a diagnosis One sign does not automatically mean a mental health disorder. A professional assessment may be worth looking into when problems persist, or when they start seriously getting in the way of everyday life.

Start by actually checking in with yourself. Notice what you're feeling without judging it. Write it down, or say it out loud to someone you trust. You don't need to have it all figured out before you talk to someone.

Reach out to a parent, guardian, teacher, counsellor, coach, or friend. If it feels easier to start somewhere private, the Get Help page has phone and text lines you can use any time.

Check in directly. Something simple like "hey, you haven't seemed like yourself lately, how are you actually doing?" goes a long way.

Listen without trying to fix it right away. Encourage them to talk to a trusted adult, and offer to go with them if that makes it easier. If you're ever worried about their safety, don't keep it a secret, get an adult involved even if they ask you not to.

Five things that make a real difference

The WHO lists healthy sleep patterns and regular exercise among the most protective habits for mental health[source].

Sleep

Try to keep a regular sleep schedule. Canadian guidelines recommend 8 to 10 hours a night for ages 14 to 17[source].

Physical activity

At least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day is the guideline[source]. Find an activity you enjoy, instead of thinking of exercise only as a workout.

Social connection

Spend time with people you trust, and talk to someone when things become difficult. See the Connection page.

Take breaks

No more than 2 hours of recreational screen time a day is the guideline[source]. Short, real breaks help more than they seem to.

Talk to someone

Spend time with people you trust and talk to someone when things become difficult, before it piles up. See Get Help.

Journal prompts

Use these in the class Wellness Journal, or anywhere private that works for you.

  2. 2Who did you actually talk to today, not just text?
  3. 3What's something you're looking forward to this week?
  4. 4When did you last feel fully relaxed? What were you doing?
  5. 5Is there something you've been putting off because it feels too big to start?
  6. 6What's one thing you could do tomorrow to make the day 10% easier?
