Being online isn't always the same as feeling connected.

This is the idea behind this whole project. Meaningful relationships protect mental health, and for a lot of teens, that's the piece that's missing.

Where does belonging fit in?

Psychologist Abraham Maslow described human needs as layers. Here's our take on where most Canadian teens sit on it.

  1. ●
    Esteem & growth
    Not the focus of this page
  2. ●
    Belonging & connection
    For many teens, only partly met
  3. ●
    Safety needs
    Mostly met for most Canadian teens
  4. ●
    Physiological needs
    Mostly met for most Canadian teens

This is our project's interpretation of how the needs pyramid applies to teens today, not a finding from a specific research study.

What the Canadian data shows

From the 2022 Health Behaviour in School-aged Children survey, Grades 6 to 10[source].

58.3%

can count on their friends when things go wrong

That also means roughly 4 in 10 feel they can't, at least not reliably.

56.3%

have friends to share both happy and sad feelings with

Again, that leaves roughly 4 in 10 without that kind of friendship right now.

Online friendships aren't automatically bad

In Grades 9 and 10, 57% of boys and 73% of girls have online contact with their close friends almost all the time during the day[source]. That's not necessarily a problem. Texting, calling, and messaging can help maintain real friendships between in-person hangouts, not replace them.

A systematic review covering 51,576 children and adolescents across 63 studies found that social isolation and loneliness increased the risk of depression, and possibly anxiety[source]. The protective factor on the other side is just as clear: supportive peer relationships protect against depression and anxiety, and buffer the effects of stress[source].

3 ways to connect for real this week

  1. 1Make an actual in-person plan with someone, even something small like walking to school together.
  2. 2Call a friend instead of texting them, just once this week.
  3. 3Do a shared activity with someone where you're actually doing something together, not just scrolling side by side.
